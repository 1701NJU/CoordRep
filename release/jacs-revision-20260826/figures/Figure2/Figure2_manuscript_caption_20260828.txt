Figure 2. Exact canonicalization eliminates input ambiguities while preserving coordination stereochemistry. (A) Canonicalization workflow. Illustrated with trans-[Co(en)2Cl2]+: chemical and attachment identities (κ-N,N; κ-Cl) are combined with rotation-invariant metric distances to refine donor ranks. Symmetry-allowed residual ties are enumerated to yield the unique, lexicographically minimum complete record. (B) End-to-end invariance challenge. Invariance benchmark across 1,000 mononuclear complexes (CN = 4–6) from tmQMg/PBE subjected to K = 100 perturbations per structure. CoordRep-State achieves 100% exact collapse across rigid-body rotation, atom reindexing, and combined perturbations (300,000/300,000 variants), whereas distance matrices and coordinate baselines fail under reindexing. (C) Whole-record minimization for exact tie resolution. In complex coordination environments containing nontrivial automorphism orbits (N = 1,000), invariant-key sorting alone achieves only 38.0% canonical collapse, whereas complete legal-tie enumeration guarantees 100.0% convergence without collapsing nonautomorphic stereoisomers. (D) Stereochemical specificity in octahedral isomers. Idealized fac- and mer-[Co(NH3)3Cl3] share identical composition, coordination number (CN = 6), and ideal Oh shape metrics, but are resolved into distinct CoordRep-States via their donor-pair relation fields: relfac = {N–Cl; N–Cl; N–Cl}trans versus relmer = {N–N; N–Cl; Cl–Cl}trans.
