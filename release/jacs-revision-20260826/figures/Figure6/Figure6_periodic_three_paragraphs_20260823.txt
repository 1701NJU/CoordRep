For periodic materials,66–68 CoordRep addresses a related but distinct identity problem. Labeled bond lengths and angles do not fully specify an oriented three-dimensional donor arrangement: two local metal environments can have identical M–D distances and D–M–D angles yet opposite orientation parity. Metric identity (MID) captures their common metric geometry from the metal/site labels and donor-vector Gram matrix. Because a Gram matrix is unchanged by reflection or inversion, MID intentionally merges these opposite-parity environments; this is appropriate for metric matching but insufficient when local stereochemical orientation must be retained. Stereo identity (SID) resolves this ambiguity by adding the signs of scalar triple products of canonically ordered donor vectors. These signs are preserved by proper rotations but reversed by reflection or inversion. CoordRep emits both keys from the same canonical local-state record, allowing metric queries to retrieve the pair together while orientation-sensitive queries retain two distinct states. Here, orientation denotes the parity of the local donor-vector frame, rather than the absolute direction of a coordination polyhedron relative to the unit cell. MID and SID are therefore not additional L0–L3 levels. L0–L3 define a nested molecular identity hierarchy from geometry-resolved records to coordination families, whereas MID and SID provide a complementary metric-versus-stereo comparison axis for periodic local states. In both cases, CoordRep makes the intended comparison resolution explicit rather than imposing a single definition of structural identity.

Among 172,332 local metal-site states in the CSD MOF Collection, 36,552 MIDs expand to 60,139 SIDs: 12,965 remain single, whereas 23,587 split into exactly two. No CN = 2 MID splits because two donor vectors cannot define an oriented three-dimensional volume, whereas 60.3–79.9% of the MIDs at CN = 3–6 split, with the highest fraction at CN = 5. Within individual structures, one MID coexists with two SIDs in 7,721 of 9,189 state-bearing entries not flagged as Sohncke (84.02%) but in none of 1,759 state-bearing entries flagged as Sohncke. This association is consistent with the availability of improper crystallographic operations in non-Sohncke space groups and their absence from Sohncke groups,69 although it does not by itself identify the symmetry operation relating each pair. Canonical invariance was then tested using 400 entries drawn from disjoint families and containing 5,920 local sites. Combined atom and donor permutations, origin shifts, periodic relabeling, and positive-determinant basis changes preserved every entry-state multiset and all MIDs and SIDs. MID also remained unchanged in 50/50 negative-determinant basis tests; SID was evaluated only under orientation-preserving transformations, consistent with its deliberately orientation-sensitive definition.

Application of CoordRep to the CSD MOF Collection thus provides a database-scale, symmetry-resolved census of local coordination-state orientation. It shows that many local metal sites indistinguishable by labeled bond-length/angle geometry become distinct when orientation parity is retained, and that the within-crystal occurrence of paired parities is strongly associated with non-Sohncke symmetry. This association does not establish continuous chirality magnitude, macroscopic framework chirality, or a property consequence. As a source-faithful representation, CoordRep inherits the coordination assignment, protonation state, disorder model, and structural resolution supplied by the CSD; it does not independently infer a unique chemical species when those source assignments remain unresolved. The periodic implementation records translation-resolved local first coordination spheres rather than whole-framework net topology. Within these boundaries, CoordRep can preserve a specific geometry-resolved record, retrieve the broader coordination family to which it belongs, and distinguish metric-equivalent periodic sites when orientation-sensitive comparison is required.
