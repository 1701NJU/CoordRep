**Figure 1. Canonical, continuous, and compositional representation of a resolved coordination state.** (A) The metal-containing component of the experimental cisplatin structure CUKRAB02 is shown as a conventional 2D formula and as its coordinate-resolved PtN\(_2\)Cl\(_2\) first coordination sphere. The corresponding CoordRep-State separates the metal context, continuous shape coordinates, donor-pair relations, and ligand/donor-site identities. At CN = 4, two trans and four cis relations account for all six unique donor pairs. The DMF crystallization solvent and hydrogen atoms are omitted from the 3D view. (B) Equivalent inputs related by rigid-body rotation and atom-order permutation converge to the same canonical CoordRep-State. Rotation-invariant metal–donor and donor–donor distances initiate canonical donor ranking; remaining exact ties are resolved by full-record minimization as detailed in Figure 2. Numbered circles denote the input donor indices, whereas L1–L4 denote the resulting canonical donor order. (C) CoSyMLib continuous shape measures compare the experimental first sphere with ideal square-planar and tetrahedral reference geometries, giving *S*(SP-4) = 0.304 and *S*(T-4) = 33.287, respectively; lower values indicate closer agreement. SP-4 and T-4 are CoSyMLib reference-shape codes, whereas *D*\(_{4h}\) and *T*\(_d\) denote the symmetries of the corresponding ideal reference geometries, not the molecular point group of the experimental cisplatin component. The reference geometries are independently rotated for visual clarity; rigid reorientation does not affect *S*. (D) The canonical record can be parsed into typed field blocks that support independent field access and comparison. Cross-field validation precedes deterministic reserialization in the fixed metal → shape → relations → ligands order.
